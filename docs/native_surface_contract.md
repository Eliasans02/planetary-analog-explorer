# Native surface browsing contract

`shared/schemas/surface_catalog.schema.json` and `shared/types/surface-catalog.ts` define a separate version 1.1.0 catalog for real, unranked Moon/Earth/Mars terrain browsing, retaining acceptance of the earlier Moon/Earth 1.0.0 contract. The existing similarity/ENU contract remains unchanged. A native surface record is not a scored `Location` and cannot satisfy `verify:assets` for the comparison engine.

The display uses actual raster pixel-center samples, a Float32 little-endian height-offset buffer, and a separate UInt8 validity mask. Height offsets equal source height minus the recorded crop display zero, in metres. Invalid entries contain zero with mask zero; they are excluded from triangle construction. Source zero-height values are valid where the source mask says they are valid. Never bridge missing cells or invent fill values.

The horizontal display frame is `native_projected_relief`. X increases with raster columns; Z increases with raster rows; Y is relative source height. A lunar stereographic grid axis is not automatically geographic east or north. The Earth geographic grid uses metre spacing evaluated at the crop center for display, with the approximation explicitly recorded. Neither display is exact Cartesian ENU or a validated body-fixed reconstruction. Native WKT, pixel-center origin, signed spacing, units and the method remain in the record/provenance.

Coordinates retain their actual source realization: MOON_ME_DE421 for the supplied lunar product and NAD83 for the supplied USGS tile. A diagram positioning their longitude and latitude is a schematic locator in that source frame; it is not a transformation into IAU_MOON or WGS84. A source body/frame mismatch must be rejected.

The NASA crop centers on valid published ROI record 3; its outline is preserved in raster-grid coordinates. Records 1 and 2 are not repaired or used. The surrounding rectangular crop is display context, not an enlargement of the published ROI. The Earth crop uses an explicit project selection rule (highest valid sample of the available tile), not a NASA-approved analogue or mission site. Crop dimensions and camera fitting are engineering choices, not scientific observations.

Source height extrema and relief are measured from valid samples of the displayed crop. Absolute height interpretation is not implied by the raw source numbers. Direct-return counts describe source sampling; published interpolated surface pixels remain distinct from direct laser measurements. Missing return-count information is null. No similarity score, habitat suitability, surface temperature, horizon, sunlight, uncertainty bound or splat reconstruction is inferred from these displays.

Every source records its actual retrieval URL, checksum, provider and license description. Public provenance sidecars retain raw input identities, source metadata and processing choices. Render assets have their own byte counts and SHA-256 hashes. The loader bounds downloads, verifies assets, rejects malformed dimensions/masks/values, and loads only local same-origin artifacts during the demonstration. Original source links are user-invoked provenance navigation, not dependencies for rendering.

Acceptance for this milestone: deterministic export from the cached real inputs; source-to-buffer spot checks; schema/semantic/hash checks; gap-safe geometry; successful build; actual browser inspection of both sources and switching. Exact ENU registration, climate comparability, similarity calibration, 3D splats and 60 FPS certification remain separate requirements.

## Derived terrain analysis

`surface_analysis` 1.0.0 is a separate offline artifact, defined by `shared/schemas/surface_analysis.schema.json` and `shared/types/surface-analysis.ts`. Its full catalog SHA-256 binds the exact native catalog response, while each record repeats its input height/mask hashes, dimensions and metre spacing. The browser must reject stale identities, missing/duplicate records, invalid metrics and inconsistent histogram counts rather than displaying approximate substitutes.

The analysis describes the displayed native raster window. Horn slopes use the two catalog metre spacings; roughness is the 3-by-3 sample range; Riley TRI is the root sum of squared differences from the central height to its eight neighbors. All local metrics require nine valid samples. Edges and neighborhoods touching masked data are excluded, with analyzed and possible interior counts reported. Relief uses all valid heights. The five slope bins represent percentages of analyzed neighborhoods, not probabilities of safe travel. A window with no eligible neighborhood has unavailable local statistics, never a fabricated zero.

These are estimates in the supplied projected grid or local metre approximation, not exact surface distances or ENU. The range and TRI include the terrain gradient and are not detrended rockiness. Source interpolation, spatial resolution, window extent and unknown error remain limitations. Values across different support scales do not constitute a calibrated similarity ranking. See `docs/research/SURFACE_ANALYSIS.md` for equations, primary references and the path to broader analysis.

## Published surface imagery

`surface_imagery` 1.0.0 is a separate, optional artifact defined by `shared/schemas/surface_imagery.schema.json` and `shared/types/surface-imagery.ts`. It binds the exact surface catalog bytes and repeats each native grid's height/mask hashes, origin, signed spacing, dimensions and CRS. Six Moon/Mars records contain six global mosaic layers and three local HiRISE orthophotos. Earth has no supplied photographic layer.

Every RGB8 PNG is sampled at the native DEM vertices from verified published imagery. Pixel centers use UV `(column + 0.5) / width, (row + 0.5) / height`, bitmap orientation `none`, and texture `flipY=false`. Published grayscale is replicated across RGB; enhanced IRB retains its published channels and is explicitly labeled false color. Initial image selection prefers the local HiRISE layer when present and otherwise uses the coarse orbital display drape; asynchronous catalog completion must preserve an explicit user selection.

Image source and lighting are separate controls. Default **Relief light** uses the source sRGB texture on a white Lambert material, without elevation vertex colors or tone mapping. Neutral ambient/key intensities of π × 0.38 and π × 0.62 account for Three r170's Lambert response: linear source brightness ranges from 0.38 fill to 1 at a surface facing the key. The fixed 30° key elevation gives a horizontal surface 0.69; the direction slider rotates in native grid axes. This is illustrative light, not a real-Sun, ephemeris, albedo or calibrated photometry claim. Original image illumination and shadows remain baked into the source pixels. **Original image · unlit** uses a white basic material without tone mapping or added illumination to preserve the published display. **Relief shading** remains a separate elevation-tinted display. Image or light changes retain geometry, camera orientation and position, collision and walking state.

Prepared orbital-color drapes use the same verified global display source as each globe; the three Mars drapes now sample the NASA-published 1440 × 720 Viking display texture, with approximate frame placement and coarse resolution; it may contain only one source color across a small window. Lunar polar source fill is not a local color photograph. Local HiRISE registration uses the companion PDS label, verified affine and geographic bounds, without claiming independent ground-control validation. The mapping and deterministic nearest-neighbor tie policy are documented in `docs/research/SURFACE_IMAGERY.md`. No new albedo, mineralogy or natural RGB measurements are inferred from these display products.

Runtime downloads are local, bounded and hash-verified. The loader checks RGB8 PNG headers before decoding and verifies decoded dimensions. Stale requests, changed sites and component cleanup close bitmaps and dispose textures. Image failures retain usable terrain and expose the failure instead of inventing source color.


The current orbital Mars presentation uses NASA's [published Viking display texture](https://science.nasa.gov/3d-resources/mars/) at Native 1440 × 720 or a downsampled 1K, with per-body quality state; it does not certify natural RGB or increase native terrain coverage. The three prepared Mars orbital-color drapes were regenerated from that verified source. Native DEM buffers, masks, local HiRISE assets and native-grid analysis retain their existing identities and geometry.
